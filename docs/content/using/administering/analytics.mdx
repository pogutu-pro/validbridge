# Analytics, Audit & Security

This page covers the controls that keep an organization healthy and accountable: analytics to see what is happening, audit logs to see who did what, and the sign-in and two-factor settings that protect accounts.

## Analytics

### Organization analytics

The dashboard's **Analytics** section gives a high-level view of the whole organization: learner activity, engagement and trends over time. Use it to answer questions like "are people coming back?", and "which courses attract attention?".

### Course analytics

Every course has an **Analytics** tab (on supported plans) showing enrolment, progress and completion. This is where you diagnose a specific course.

### Assignment analytics

Each assignment has its own analytics for completion and scores.

### Per-user analytics

Open a user under **Users** to see their personal learning activity — useful when supporting someone with a specific problem.

## Audit logs

**Audit logs** record important actions in the organization: who changed a setting, who was given a role, who published a course. When something unexpected happened, the audit log tells you what changed and who changed it.

- Open **Users → Audit logs**.
- Filter or scan by time and actor.
- Use it during an incident to reconstruct events.

<Callout type="info">
Audit logs are append-only records. They are evidence, not a working area — do not expect to edit them.
</Callout>

## Sign-in methods

The **Sign-in methods** tab controls how users may authenticate:

- **Email and password** — the default.
- **Google** — social sign-in, convenient for many users.
- **SSO** — enterprise single sign-on, configured under [Developers](/using/administering/developers).

Turning a method on or off affects everyone. If you remove a method people rely on, tell them first.

## Two-factor authentication

The **Two-factor** tab sets the organization's two-factor **policy**:

- **Optional** — users may enable 2FA themselves.
- **Required** — everyone must use 2FA to sign in.

Requiring 2FA is one of the highest-value security changes an administrator can make. Do it deliberately, and give people notice and instructions for enrolling an authenticator app.

## A practical security checklist

- [ ] Require two-factor authentication.
- [ ] Keep the number of administrators small.
- [ ] Disable the sign-in methods you do not use.
- [ ] Give people the least role they need.
- [ ] Rotate API keys and revoke unused ones.
- [ ] Review the audit log after any unusual event.
- [ ] Remove access for people who have left.
